Research Projects

I am a vertebrate paleontologist and Ph.D. candidate at the University of Florida whose research integrates museum collections, morphology, paleobiology, and evolutionary history to investigate patterns of mammalian diversity and adaptation through the Cenozoic. My work focuses on fossil mammals and the use of quantitative approaches, including geometric morphometrics, Bayesian phylogenetics, and AI to address questions in evolution, ecology, and functional morphology. In parallel, I develop and coordinate programs that connect scientists, educators, students, and communities through authentic STEM experiences — expanding access to Earth and environmental sciences across Florida.

Fossil Horse Macroevolution

Investigating macroevolutionary transitions in Neogene Equidae using phenomics and machine learning

Fossil horses (Family Equidae) provide one of the most complete records of mammalian evolution in the fossil record, spanning over 55 million years in North America. My dissertation research focuses on macroevolutionary transitions in Neogene fossil horses, leveraging phenomics — the large-scale, quantitative measurement of phenotypic variation — alongside machine learning to detect patterns of evolutionary change across time and space. By integrating museum collections from across North America with computational approaches, I aim to reveal how populations of fossil horses changed morphologically across major climatic and environmental transitions.

A central component of this work involves applying interpretable deep learning models to images of fossil horse teeth, which preserve detailed morphological information about diet, ecology, and phylogenetic relationships. This approach allows me to quantify subtle variation across large sample sizes that would be impractical to measure manually, opening new windows into paleopopulation structure and evolutionary dynamics. This work was recognized with a Romer Prize Session presentation at the 2026 Society of Vertebrate Paleontology meeting.

I also investigate species occurrence patterns of Late Miocene horses from Florida, examining whether gaps in the fossil record reflect genuine ecological absences, sampling biases, or both — an important methodological question for interpreting evolutionary and biogeographic patterns from museum collections.

Strontium Isotope Geochemistry & REEs

Using fossil shark teeth as geochemical archives to calibrate Neogene chronology

Fossil shark teeth are remarkably durable and abundant in the fossil record, and they preserve geochemical signatures that record the chemistry of ancient seawater. My MS research focused on using strontium (Sr) isotope ratios and rare earth elements (REEs) preserved in fossil shark teeth as age proxies for Neogene fossil sites across Florida. Because marine Sr isotope ratios have changed predictably through geologic time, teeth that preserve primary geochemical signatures can be matched to a global Sr isotope curve to estimate the age of the deposit in which they were found.

This work demonstrated that marine strontium isotopes preserved in fossil shark teeth can calibrate Neogene land mammal evolution, providing chronological anchors for fossil sites that lack other reliable dating material. The approach has broad implications for improving the temporal resolution of Florida's rich Neogene fossil record and for calibrating biostratigraphic frameworks across the southeastern United States. I also investigated REE uptake patterns to assess diagenetic alteration and evaluate which specimens retain reliable primary geochemical signals.

AI & Computational Tools

Applying machine learning and quantitative methods to paleontological and biological data

Artificial intelligence and machine learning are transforming how paleontologists can interrogate large morphological datasets. My research applies deep learning models — including convolutional neural networks and interpretable AI approaches — to images of vertebrate fossils to classify taxa, quantify morphological variation, and detect macroevolutionary patterns at scales previously impossible with manual measurement. A key focus of this work is understanding how sample size, taxonomic diversity, and image quality affect model performance, with practical implications for museum digitization and collections-based research.

The Shark AI Project

Alongside fossil horse research, I have contributed to the SharkAI project, which uses fossil shark teeth as a tangible, accessible dataset for building and evaluating machine learning image classification models. This project bridges cutting-edge computational methods with museum collections, producing tools and curricula usable by both researchers and K-12 students. My work here also contributes to broader conversations about bias in AI training data, model interpretability, and the use of natural history collections as computational resources.

Chewing on Change — Module 4: AI & Machine Learning

Chewing on Change is a K-12 curriculum on the macroevolution of horses in response to climate change, developed in partnership with the Florida Museum of Natural History. Module 4 introduces students to machine learning by having them use real fossil horse tooth images and a cloud-based Jupyter notebook on mybinder.org — no coding experience required. Students train and evaluate an image classification model, explore how AI identifies patterns in fossil data, and discuss the broader implications of AI across science disciplines through a jigsaw reading activity.

Classroom materials include a full curriculum PDF, teaching slides, a video walkthrough, and an under-the-hood code explainer to support teachers and students who want to go deeper. A curated set of readings on AI applications across disciplines and areas of concern — including water use, energy costs, and data center ethics — are available for classroom discussion.

This module is part of an IRB-approved research study (UF-IRB202600133: Chewing on Change: Using AI to Study Fossil Horse Teeth) investigating how student and teacher understanding of macroevolutionary traits, AI/ML literacy, attitudes about science, and self-efficacy for using AI change after participating in the lesson. The study is funded by the National Science Foundation (NSF2147625) and is currently being implemented in middle and high school classrooms across Florida, Georgia, and Ohio.

Education & Broader Impacts

Connecting scientific research with educators, students, and communities across Florida

Science is most powerful when it is accessible. As the coordinator for the Scientist in Every Florida School (SEFS) program at the University of Florida Thompson Earth Systems Institute and Florida Museum of Natural History, I develop and manage programs that place scientists directly into K-12 classrooms and build lasting partnerships between researchers and educators. This work spans teacher professional development, research-based curriculum design, scientist-classroom partnerships, and outreach programming that expands access to Earth and environmental sciences across Florida — particularly in underserved and rural communities. Since 2019, I have spearheaded, implemented, managed, and coordinated over 20 professional development programs connecting K-12 teachers with cutting-edge research across paleontology, AI, ecology, plant biology, and environmental science.

Montbrook Teacher Field Experiences

One of SEFS's most powerful teacher professional development experiences takes place at the Montbrook fossil site in Levy County, Florida — one of the most productive late Miocene vertebrate fossil localities in the southeastern United States. Teachers participate in authentic paleontological fieldwork alongside museum scientists, excavating and identifying fossil specimens in the field. This immersive experience is designed to give teachers firsthand exposure to scientific methods, helping them bring the nature of science to life in their own classrooms. Lessons developed around this experience connect fossil collection, stratigraphic context, and evolutionary history directly to K-12 science standards.
